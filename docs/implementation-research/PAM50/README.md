# Multimodal PAM50 subtype prediction — surveyed papers, methods and metrics

Literature search run **2026-07-31**. Question: *who has published multimodal machine learning for
PAM50 intrinsic-subtype prediction in breast cancer, on what data, with what pipeline, and what did
they actually report?*

The deliverable is the three tables in §3. Everything else exists to make those tables safe to cite:
§2 says how each cell was obtained, §4–§7 say what the numbers do and do not mean, §8 lists what was
checked and deliberately left out, §9 records where the search itself is weak. Full verbatim fields
per paper live in **[`paper-dossier.md`](paper-dossier.md)**; the machine-readable payload is
**[`survey-data.json`](survey-data.json)**.

---

## 1. Findings that change how the table should be read

1. **The cell this project occupies — H&E WSI + bulk RNA → PAM50 — is nearly empty.** Eight papers of
   the 50 surveyed do it. Two collapse PAM50 to binary, one reports no extractable numbers at all,
   one never names a cohort or a sample size, one names neither its class count nor which TCGA
   project it used. **Exactly two — Benkirane 2025 and Shah & Patel 2026 — report a multi-class PAM50
   AUROC on a named cohort under a stated protocol, and neither states how many classes it is
   predicting.** The scarcity is the finding.

2. **Gated fusion — the operator in this repo — appears in exactly two papers, and it loses in both.**
   Hezil 2026a: gated 85.12% vs RNA-only 92.11%. Rajalekshmi 2026, verbatim: "neither early
   concatenation, gated fusion, nor attention-based fusion provided measurable improvements over
   transcriptomic features alone."

3. **"Fusion does not beat RNA-only" is the field's modal result, not this project's anomaly.** Four
   independent groups report it (Rajalekshmi 2026, Hezil 2026a, Abdullakutty 2026, Benkirane 2025 —
   whose entire fusion gain is 0.4 points inside one standard deviation). What no one has published
   is *why*; the ablation showing the gate decides on RNA alone is the mechanism nobody else has shown
   for a result four other groups have already observed.
   This is *not* because fusion is useless. MDL-IIA (npj Breast Cancer 2023) fuses mammography with
   ultrasound and beats both unimodal arms decisively — MCC 0.837 vs 0.731 and 0.744, p < 0.001, on a
   672-case held-out test with CIs on every figure. The difference is structural: its two modalities
   are comparably weak and genuinely complementary, whereas in imaging+RNA→PAM50 one modality is very
   nearly the assay that defines the label, so any competent gate learns to ignore the image.

4. **No published multimodal PAM50 model has an external, never-trained-on, PAM50-specific evaluation.**
   Six of the eight H&E+RNA papers have no external cohort at all. M3FusionNet has CPTAC but reports
   only a pooled "Overall AUC 0.82" aggregated across three binary receptor tasks, one multiclass task
   and one regression — no PAM50-specific external number appears anywhere in it.

5. **HER2-enriched collapse under domain shift is the field norm.** Fernandez-Romero 2026 reports
   "complete performance collapse (RPD = 1.000 across all models)" for HER2-enriched on CPTAC, and
   attributes 80.0% of degradation variance to staining plus feature-space divergence, with prevalence
   shift *not* significant. An external HER2-E recall of 0.00 is the expected result on this transfer,
   and the remediation it points at is normalisation and encoder choice, not class reweighting.

6. **Two citation habits in this area are not supported by the source texts** (each verified here by
   full-text grep, not by abstract):
   - **TANGLE and THREADS never evaluate on PAM50.** TANGLE's breast task is IDC vs ILC; THREADS'
     single "PAM50" hit is an Extended Data table caption. The canonical WSI+transcriptomics
     pretraining line has no PAM50 result to cite.
   - **HE2RNA, SEQUOIA and Path2Omics report no PAM50 subtype endpoint.** SEQUOIA uses PAM50 as a
     gene-overlap statistic and a Cox covariate; Path2Omics' full preprint text contains zero
     occurrences of "PAM50"; HE2RNA's worked example is MSI. Citing them as PAM50-from-histology
     precedent is a misreading.

7. **The RNA-as-input-and-label circularity is pervasive and unmeasured — but it is not automatic.**
   PAM50 *is* a 50-gene expression centroid correlation, so any RNA-input model partly re-derives the
   assay from its own substrate, and the direct-read omics models cluster at 0.96–0.98 AUROC. Two rows
   bracket that ceiling and show it is a property of *access* to the expression vector, not of omics
   per se: OmicsFootPrint, which renders each omics layer as a ring of a composite image and makes a
   CNN read the picture, drops to **0.83 ± 0.07**; hist2RNA, whose expression is *predicted* from H&E
   rather than measured, falls to **0.55 accuracy / 0.54 F1** despite recovering 13 of its 20
   best-predicted genes from the PAM50 set. **Not one paper in this table reports excluding the 50
   PAM50 genes from its transcriptomic input.** The decisive experiment is unpublished — and cheap.

8. **Radiology → PAM50 exists but is one cohort published twice.** Both Ming 2022 papers are the same
   group on the same single-institution 174-patient Nanjing cohort. The accuracy-only paper reports
   PAM50 at 0.467 rising to 0.742 once a 10 mm peritumour ring is added — 5-fold CV, no held-out set,
   class count `NR`. The radiomics paper is the only radiology work here with a genuine external test
   (n=72, different scanner), and it does it as four one-vs-rest binaries at AUC 0.733–0.809. Plus one
   graph model (Furtney 2023). That is the entire evidence base.

9. **Nothing in this literature is directly comparable to anything else in it.** Class count varies
   (4 vs 5) and is simply not reported in six papers; one paper contradicts itself (4-class in the
   text, `NUM_CLASSES = 5` in its own released code); prediction unit varies across patch, slide and
   patient and is worth ~9 accuracy points where it has been measured; label basis flips between
   transcriptomic PAM50 and IHC surrogate, sometimes *within one cohort*. §5 names the offenders.

## 2. How this was produced, and how far to trust it

**Search.** Two multi-agent workflows, 17 subagents, ~1.9M agent tokens, ~700 tool calls, all on
2026-07-31. Six parallel searchers (biomedical registries; CS preprints; Google Scholar + open web;
unimodal baselines; expression-inference and radiogenomics; citation-chaining), then per-paper field
extraction, then a coverage critic that had to *verify* each suspected gap before reporting it, then
synthesis. A second workflow extracted the eight papers the critic recovered and resolved two
unverifiable DOIs. 59 unique candidates were found; 42 went to extraction and 8 more were added by
the critic pass; 17 low-priority candidates were dropped and are listed in §9.

**Registries actually used:** PubMed, Europe PMC, arXiv, OpenAlex, Crossref, Google Scholar, CORE,
plus direct web search and page fetches for conference proceedings and repositories. `semantic`,
`base`, `dblp`, `ssrn` returned nothing through this tooling; `biorxiv` and `pmc` returned a recency
feed rather than query-relevant results and were abandoned. Paywalls that blocked verification are
itemised in §9 — IEEE (HTTP 418), ScienceDirect (403), Springer (IdP redirect), Nature (auth wall).

**Integrity rules the extracting agents worked under.** No invented metrics, cohort sizes, venues or
DOIs; `NR` wherever a value was not seen; every link verified to resolve to the claimed paper; every
row carries an evidence level. Numbers were copied verbatim with their metric name and split type and
were never converted, rounded or averaged. Of the 50 rows, **42 are full-text reads and 8 are
abstract-only** — the `Ev.` column says which, per paper. 11 of the 50 are unrefereed preprints, and
the set spans 2018–2026 with 27 of 50 published in 2025 or 2026.

**What this still is not.** An abstract-only row means the pipeline and metric details in it are as
thin as the abstract was, not that the paper is thin. Several venue strings were inferred from DOI
prefixes rather than read off a publisher page (flagged in the dossier). Two IEEE conference papers
that look directly competitive could not be verified past their abstracts at all. Treat every
unrefereed preprint in the tables as unrefereed — they are labelled `PREPRINT`.

## 3. The tables

**Reading the cells.**

- **`Modality (model inputs)`** — what the model actually *consumes at inference*, assigned by hand
  from each paper's own modality description. This matters because that description is prose full of
  negations ("no clinical variables", "RNA only defines the labels") that keyword rules read wrong,
  and because a paper titled "multimodal" is routinely omics-only with no imaging anywhere in it.
- **Long cells are trimmed at a sentence or clause boundary and end in `…`.** The full verbatim text
  of every field is in [`paper-dossier.md`](paper-dossier.md); the paper ID in column 1 links straight
  to it. Nothing is paraphrased in the trimming — only cut.
- **`NR`** = not reported by the paper, or not visible at the evidence level reached. It is never a
  stand-in for a value that was seen.
- **`Ev.`** = evidence level: `FT` full text read, `AB` abstract only, `MD` metadata only.
- Tier assignment (`core` / `adjacent` / `background`) is the extracting agent's, based on the
  endpoint the paper actually reports. Where the tier and the modality column seem to disagree, the
  `Endpoint` cell says why — those disagreements are left visible rather than smoothed over. **One
  tier was overridden here:** hist2RNA came back tagged `core`, but its own Fusion field reads
  "unimodal (H&E only)" — it is a WSI → predicted-expression → subtype cascade, so it sits in
  `background` with the rest of that tier.

50 papers: 18 core, 23 adjacent, 9 background.

## 4. Landscape
**What the field has actually built.** Of the 50 surveyed papers (18 core, 23 adjacent, 9 background), 25 fuse two or more modalities at inference time, 24 are unimodal at inference (counting Wang W 2026, where RNA is a training-time teacher only, and Hezil 2026b (IEEE JBHI), whose "modality-disjoint" design never fuses within a patient), and one is a review with no experiments of its own (Kunhoth 2024).

Modality combinations, by count:

| Combination | n | Papers |
|---|---|---|

| H&E WSI + bulk transcriptomics | 8 | Hezil 2026a (Med Eng Phys), Shah & Patel 2026, Hezil 2026b (JBHI), Abdullakutty 2026, Benkirane 2025, Shandiz 2025, Shukla 2025, Zhang Z 2024 |

(Furtney 2023 is the only paper counted in two rows; every other row is disjoint, and the rows sum to 50.)

The **H&E + RNA** cell — the one this project occupies — is thinner than it looks. Of those 8 papers: two collapse PAM50 to binary (Shandiz 2025, Abdullakutty 2026, sharing the identical `BRCA.Luminal` vs `BRCA.Basal/Her2` label space); one reports no extractable numbers at all (Zhang Z 2024, "up to 10.1% improvement" on unnamed metrics); one never names a cohort or a sample size (Hezil 2026b); one names neither its class count nor which TCGA project it used (Shukla 2025, "1,081 breast cancer patients", accuracy 87% / recall 87%). **Exactly two of the eight report a multi-class PAM50 AUROC on a named cohort under a stated protocol — Benkirane 2025, and Shah & Patel 2026 (PAM50 macro-AUC 0.96 on TCGA-BRCA, n ≈ 1,036, protocol E1) — and neither states how many classes it is predicting.**

**Fusion stage.** Intermediate/latent fusion dominates (~13 of 25): bilinear + mixture-of-experts (Benkirane 2025), mixed-integration VAE (Benkirane 2023), cross-modal transformer tokens (Liu S & Min 2026), R-GCN cross-omics message passing (Li C 2026), hypergraph + supervised contrastive (Bhole 2025), per-omics attention (Choi & Chae 2023), similarity-network fusion (Ahmad 2026), graph message passing over a patient graph (Furtney 2023), cross-attention (Shandiz 2025, Zhang Z 2024, Huang 2023), stacked intra- then inter-modality self-attention (Zhang T 2023), and hypernetwork weight generation (Abdullakutty 2026, the deepest operator in the table). Early concatenation is the next largest group (Shah & Patel 2026 → XGBoost/CatBoost, Ben Rabah 2025, Omran 2025, Patel 2025, Li & Nabavi 2024, and Tang X 2024, which concatenates in *image* space by drawing each omics layer as a concentric ring of one composite picture). Genuine late/decision-level fusion is rare — three papers: label-space VCDN (Wang T 2021), per-modality weighted logits (Amer 2025), and grade-thresholded decision rules (Lee 2024).

**Gated fusion — the operator this project uses — appears in exactly two papers, and it loses in both.** Hezil 2026a reports gated fusion at 85.12% accuracy against RNA-only at 92.11%; Rajalekshmi 2026 states outright that "neither early concatenation, gated fusion, nor attention-based fusion provided measurable improvements over transcriptomic features alone."

**Radiology → PAM50 is not absent, it is small and weak.** Three papers predict a PAM50-style label from radiology. Ming 2022a and Ming 2022b are the same group on the same single-institution 174-patient Nanjing cohort (DCE-MRI + paired frozen-tumour RNA-seq, RNA used only to make labels). Ming 2022a reports PAM50 **only as accuracy, never AUROC**: 0.467 (95% CI 0.462–0.470) from the tumour region alone with Xception, rising to 0.742 (0.738–0.746) with Inception-v3 once a 10 mm peritumour ring is added — 5-fold CV on 174 patients, no held-out set, no external set, class count NR. Ming 2022b converts PAM50 into four one-vs-rest binary radiomics classifiers and is the only radiology paper here with a genuine external test (n=72, TCGA-BRCA/TCIA, 1.5T GE against a Siemens 3T discovery cohort, internal test n=52): LumA AUC 0.8252 → 0.733, LumB 0.7673 → 0.7354, HER2E 0.7933 → 0.809, Basal 0.7751 → 0.7742 — the LumB and HER2E classifiers use 5 and 2 selected features respectively. Furtney 2023 folds DCE-MRI into a multi-relational patient graph alongside IHC, clinical and genomic nodes. That is the entire radiology→PAM50 evidence base: one 174-patient cohort published twice, plus one graph model. Nothing in it approaches the H&E literature in sample size, and only one of the numbers is a real AUC on an independent scanner.

**The one clean multimodal-imaging win.** Zhang T 2023 (MDL-IIA) fuses mammography (CC + MLO) with ultrasound on 3,360 paired cases (train 2,688 / held-out test 672) and reports 4-category subtype accuracy 88.5% [86.0, 90.9], F1 0.862 [0.831, 0.892], MCC 0.837 [0.803, 0.870] — beating US-only ResNet50 (81.1%, MCC 0.731), mammography-only ResNet50 (82.0%, MCC 0.744) and a naive multimodal ResNet50 (84.4%, MCC 0.777), both unimodal comparisons at p < 0.001. This is the most convincingly powered fusion-beats-unimodal result in the table. It is also not PAM50: the string "PAM50" does not appear anywhere in the paper, the labels are IHC surrogates (ER/PR/HER2/Ki67 with SISH for equivocal HER2), the study is single-centre with one mammography system and one ultrasound system, and there is no external cohort. Its relevance to us is structural, not numerical — see Section 3.

**The WSI → expression cascade is its own tier.** Three background papers predict transcript levels from H&E rather than subtype directly: Wang Y 2021 (Cancer Research), Pizurica 2024 (SEQUOIA) and Mondol 2023 (hist2RNA). Only hist2RNA closes the loop to a subtype call, and it does so by cascade: WSI → predicted PAM50 genes → soft-voting classifier. Wang W 2026 sits nearby but does the opposite: it predicts subtype *directly* — binary MSI vs MSS and GBM Proneural vs Mesenchymal, on colorectal, gastric and glioma cohorts, not breast — using RNA only as a training-time alignment target.

**Dataset concentration.** 35 of 50 rows touch TCGA-BRCA. CPTAC-BRCA appears in seven (Shah & Patel 2026, Fernandez-Romero 2026, Borji 2026, Zhang X 2025, Tafavvoghi 2025, Liu H 2024, Pizurica 2024); in one of those (Tafavvoghi 2025) it is pooled into training rather than held out, and in two more (Liu H 2024, Pizurica 2024) it is used only for the gene-expression endpoint, never for PAM50. METABRIC appears in two (Liu S & Min 2026 as a primary cohort, n=1,981; Qiu 2026 as an external target, n=1,980), CBCS three times (Kaczmarzyk 2026, Lee 2024, Couture 2018). The implication is structural, not incidental: **matched WSI + RNA + PAM50 exists at scale in precisely one cohort, and the only public second cohort is ~120–134 patients.** Borji 2026's CPTAC split contains 12 HER2-enriched slides; Fernandez-Romero 2026's contains 120 patients total. Every external PAM50 number in this literature is therefore a ~100-patient test with a single-digit-to-low-double-digit minority class, which is why per-class external estimates are uninterpretable everywhere including here.

## 5. Metric comparability
The headline numbers in this table cannot be ranked against each other. Five separate reasons, each with named offenders.

**1. The label space is not the same object across papers, seven never state it, and two contradict themselves.** Normal-like is dropped in Ektefaie 2021, Benkirane 2023, Amer 2025, Patel 2025, Tafavvoghi 2025, Borji 2026, Jaber 2020 (explicitly, at n=23), Li & Nabavi 2024, Phan 2021, Kurian 2025, Qiu 2026, Mondol 2023 and Tang X 2024 — the last being the only paper to give a stated rationale, excluding Normal-like calls "as potentially low-quality tumour samples". It is retained in Wang T 2021 (n=115), Choi & Chae 2023 (n=40), Bhole 2025 (n=44), Li C 2026 (n=44), Omran 2025 (n=35), Furtney 2023 (n=37) and Fernandez-Romero 2026. The class count is simply **not reported** in Shah & Patel 2026, Hezil 2026b, Shukla 2025, Zhang X 2025, Zhang Z 2024, Ming 2022a and — in a PLOS Computational Biology paper — Benkirane 2025. Two papers contradict themselves: Hezil 2026a's text says 4-class with Normal-like excluded while the authors' released code sets `NUM_CLASSES = 5` with `'Normal'` in the class list, and Liu H 2024's Methods say "the four output nodes correspond to four molecular subtypes" while its Results claim "high performance in predicting all five subtypes". Liu S & Min 2026 trains a 6-class METABRIC space including Claudin-low and then evaluates externally against 5-class TCGA PAM50, and never reconciles the mismatch. Ming 2022b's cohort is labelled with five genefu classes including 10 Normal-like cases, yet the reported classifiers are four one-vs-rest tasks plus a 4-class network, with no statement of where the Normal-like cases went. And several "molecular subtyping" papers are not PAM50 at all: Liu Hong 2022, Kiraz 2025, Li B 2026, Huang 2023, Ben Rabah 2025 and Zhang T 2023 use IHC surrogates; Jimenez-Martin 2024 never states label provenance; Li & Nabavi 2024 never uses the string "PAM50" (labels are Xena's subtype field).

Even the same cohort does not yield the same class counts. TCGA-BRCA "n" for a PAM50 task ranges from 388 slides (Phan 2021) through 495 WSIs (Mondol 2023), 836 samples (Tang X 2024), 1,022 (Benkirane 2023), 1,059 (Li C 2026) and 1,522 slides / 1,079 patients (Fernandez-Romero 2026) up to 1,983 slides / 1,099 patients (Ektefaie 2021), with Liu H 2024 running on 1,979 frozen plus 1,131 FFPE WSIs — a five-fold spread on one cohort for one task. And Choi & Chae 2023's breakdown (HER2-enriched 182, Basal-like 81) is transposed relative to every other TCGA-BRCA breakdown in the table.

**2. Prediction unit is uncontrolled, and the spread is worth ~10 points.** Jaber 2020 is the only paper reporting all three levels for one model: patch 58.62%, WSI 66.1%, patient 67.27%. Phan 2021 goes from patch-wise 0.78 accuracy to slide-wise 0.913 — and its split is described only in patches (1,833,889 divided 70/5/25), with no statement of patient-level grouping. **Borji 2026 is the worst offender for our purposes**: its external CPTAC table is indexed by a "Number of patches" column at exactly 10,000 patches per class, so its external macro AUC of 0.9523 is a patch-balanced measurement, not a case-level one — it is not comparable to any case-level external number, including ours. Ben Rabah 2025 splits image-wise, so a patient's images can straddle train and test. Wang Y 2021 is the counter-example of how to do it: patient-wise splits throughout, with an explicit tile → slide → patient averaging step ("EMO-average") stated as part of the method.

**3. Split protocol ranges from 10-fold CV to a 138-sample single draw.** Hezil 2026a's headline 94.93% is a single stratified split with n=138 test samples, no cross-validation and no confidence intervals — a handful of cases moves it a percentage point. Patel 2025's held-out test set contains 4 HER2-positive patients (its reported HER2 recall of 0.6667 is not expressible over four cases), and the Methods say 5-fold CV while §3.3 says 10-fold. Qiu 2026 reports "70/15/15 split" and "fivefold cross-validation" together without saying which produced which number. Zhang T 2023's 88.5% is a single 80/20 held-out split of one centre with no cross-validation. Against these, Amer 2025 (10-fold CV), Kiraz 2025 (3×5-fold), Li C 2026 (repeated stratified 5-fold ×5) and Tang X 2024 (median over 10 random 70/15/15 re-partitions) are doing something categorically more reliable — and their numbers look *worse* as a direct consequence.

**4. Class-imbalance handling and averaging scheme move numbers more than architectures do.** Furtney 2023 reports micro AUC-ROC 0.871 and macro 0.772 for the *same model on the same data* — 10 points, purely from the averaging scheme. Jaber 2020's binary Basal classifier goes from sensitivity 38.64% to 68.18% by rebalancing alone, at essentially unchanged AUC (0.8259 → 0.8607). Tang X 2024 is the clearest demonstration of what a single averaged headline hides: its per-class AUCs are Basal 0.98 ± 0.02, HER2 0.89 ± 0.07, LumA 0.82 ± 0.03, LumB 0.74 ± 0.04 — a 24-point spread compressed into one "Overall 0.83 ± 0.07". Patel 2025 inflates training to 1,536 via ADASYN; Choi & Chae 2023 uses CVAE oversampling. Meanwhile the averaging scheme is **not reported** in Li C 2026, Benkirane 2025, Li & Nabavi 2024, Omran 2025 (a 5-class imbalanced F1 with no stated averaging), Shukla 2025, Zhang X 2025, Kiraz 2025 and Ben Rabah 2025. Shah & Patel 2026 goes further and pools: its external "Overall AUC 0.82" is aggregated across three binary receptor tasks, one multiclass task and one continuous regression — it is not a PAM50 number at all, and no PAM50-specific external number is given anywhere in the abstract.

**5. RNA-as-input-and-label circularity is pervasive and unmeasured — but it is not automatic.** PAM50 *is* a 50-gene expression centroid correlation. Any model taking bulk RNA as input and predicting PAM50 is partly re-deriving the assay from its own substrate, and most of the numbers show it: Benkirane 2023 omics-only ROC-AUC 0.9828; Rajalekshmi 2026 molecular-only AUC 0.97–0.99; Hezil 2026a RNA-only 92.11% accuracy with its router handing 61.8% of cases to the RNA head at 97.6% accuracy; Bhole 2025's mRNA-only ≈0.89 accuracy against 0.901 for all three omics; Li C 2026's mRNA-only 0.842 against 0.912 full.

**Tang X 2024 (OmicsFootPrint) breaks this pattern and the discrepancy is informative rather than dismissible.** It is multi-omics → 4-class PAM50 on TCGA-BRCA (836 samples, Normal-like excluded) and reports overall AUC **0.83 ± 0.07** — roughly 15 points below Benkirane 2023 and Rajalekshmi 2026 on nominally the same task and the same cohort family. The honest reading is not that circularity is imaginary; it is that the circularity ceiling depends on *how directly the model can read the expression vector*. OmicsFootPrint never gives its classifier the expression vector: each omics layer is rendered as a concentric ring of a composite image and a CNN is trained on the picture, so the 50-gene centroid has to survive a lossy visual re-encoding first. Its per-class profile is exactly what that predicts — Basal 0.98 (separable under almost any encoding), HER2 0.89, and the LumA/LumB boundary collapsing to 0.82/0.74, which is precisely the boundary that the PAM50 centroid distance itself resolves. So the 0.90+ cluster is not evidence that omics models are good; it is evidence that a model reading the expression vector directly is measuring rather than predicting, and Tang X 2024 quantifies the cost of interposing anything between the model and that vector. Our RNA-only 0.988 sits with the direct-read cluster and should be interpreted as such.

The reverse direction closes the argument. Mondol 2023 (hist2RNA) predicts the PAM50 gene set from H&E and then classifies from the *predicted* expression: 4-class accuracy **0.55**, F1 **0.54**, per-class AUROC 0.79 (LumA) / 0.63 (LumB) / 0.89 (Basal) / 0.78 (HER2). Thirteen of its twenty best-predicted genes belong to the PAM50 set, so the failure is not that the model ignores the right genes — it is that predicted expression is not measured expression. Take away the assay and the RNA advantage evaporates entirely.

**Not one row in this table reports excluding the 50 PAM50 genes (or their proxies) from the transcriptomic input.** Ektefaie 2021's post-hoc analysis is the cautionary data point: ESR1 expression alone gives ER AUC 0.883. Consequently, comparing an H&E-only AUROC to any direct-read RNA AUROC is comparing prediction to partial measurement. Borji 2026 compounds this from the other direction, labelling 426 of 627 TCGA WSIs from transcriptomic PAM50 and the remaining 201 from IHC surrogate rules, then reporting one macro number over the mixture.

## 6. Where this project sits
**The WSI-only result (0.888 internal / 0.847 external CPTAC, case-level macro AUROC).**

Fair comparators — same modality, same-ish label space, patient- or slide-level:

- **Amer 2025** is the cleanest internal comparator: 4-class TCGA-BRCA, patient-level, 10-fold CV, WSI-image-only macro-AUC **0.8080**. Our 0.888 is ~8 points above it on an equivalent protocol.
- **Benkirane 2025** reports WSI-only AttnMIL at **71.2 ± 4.9** and best WSI-only (CustOmics) **73.2 ± 3.1** AUC on TCGA-BRCA under stratified 5-fold. Same architecture family as ours. The 15-point gap is almost certainly the encoder, not the MIL head — and that reading is directly corroborated by **Fernandez-Romero 2026**, where switching the feature extractor moves PAM50 macro-F1 from 0.342 (ResNet-50) to 0.575 (UNI-2) with the MIL architecture held fixed.
- **Liu H 2024** reports slide-level average ROC-AUC **0.875** on TCGA-BRCA — numerically nearest to our 0.888, but slide-level with patient-level splitting NR, and the paper cannot decide whether it is a 4- or 5-class problem, so it is optimistically biased relative to a case-level number by an unknown amount.
- **Zhang X 2025 (BBMIL)** is the most honest external WSI-only PAM50 number in the table: internal TCGA AUC **0.803** → external **0.6515**. Class count NR and the external PAM50 cohort is never identified, but the *shape* — a large drop on transfer — matches ours, and their absolute level is below ours in both regimes.
- **Mondol 2023 (hist2RNA)** is the relevant weak baseline for the cascade alternative to direct MIL: WSI → predicted PAM50 genes → soft-voting ensemble, 4-class on a TCGA held-out test of 160 patients, accuracy 0.55 / F1 0.54, per-class AUROC 0.79 / 0.63 / 0.89 / 0.78. Every one of those four per-class AUROCs is below our 0.888 macro. Predicting the transcriptome and then classifying it is strictly worse than classifying the image directly, at least at this encoder scale.
- **Kunhoth 2024's** review tabulates the same ceiling from an independent hand: InceptionV3 + multiclass SVM on TCGA (1,097 patients / 1,142 slides) at WSI 66.1% / patient 67.27% accuracy; ResNet-50 on Xiangya (1,254) at 64.3% accuracy; ResNet-18 across TCGA + 6 private cohorts (3,672 slides) at a maximum TCGA AUC of **0.803**. The surveyed H&E molecular-subtyping field sits at 64–67% accuracy and ≤0.803 AUC; our 0.888 is above all of it, and the review's own conclusion is that the field's blocking weakness is the absence of external and multi-cohort validation.
- **Fernandez-Romero 2026** is the closest methodological sibling overall: UNI-2 features, CLAM, TCGA → CPTAC hold-out, patient-stratified splits. Its metric is 5-class macro-F1 (0.575 internal → 0.325 external), not AUROC, so it is not numerically comparable — but its **per-class finding is the single most important corroboration available to us: HER2-enriched shows "complete performance collapse (RPD = 1.000 across all models)" on CPTAC.** Our external HER2-enriched recall of 0.00 is the field norm on this transfer, not a pipeline defect. Their domain-shift regression further attributes 80.0% of degradation variance to staining variability plus feature-space divergence, with **prevalence shift not significant** — which argues our 0.888 → 0.847 drop is a stain/feature-space problem, not a class-prior problem, and points the remediation at normalisation and encoder choice rather than reweighting. Note also that they excluded FFPE from TCGA to match CPTAC's flash-frozen material; whether our pipeline did the same is a concrete, checkable difference that must be stated.

Not fair comparators, and why:

- **Borji 2026** (TCGA → CPTAC, 4-class, external macro AUC **0.9523**) appears to beat us by ten points and does not: it is patch-indexed at 10,000 patches per class, uses a mixed transcriptomic/IHC label set, and reports no patient counts for either cohort.
- **Zhang T 2023 (MDL-IIA)**, 4-category accuracy **88.5%** / MCC 0.837, is IHC-surrogate labels from mammography + ultrasound at one centre with no external cohort — a different label, a different modality pair and a different generalisation regime.
- **Ming 2022a**'s PAM50 accuracy of **0.742** is DCE-MRI, 5-fold CV on 174 patients, no class count, no AUROC; **Ming 2022b**'s PAM50 AUCs (0.733–0.809 external) are four separate one-vs-rest binary tasks on 72 patients, the LumB and HER2E classifiers resting on 5 and 2 selected features respectively. Neither is a multi-class macro AUROC.
- **Phan 2021** slide-wise accuracy 0.913 — patch-level split, no stated patient grouping.
- **Tafavvoghi 2025** macro-F1 0.727 — CPTAC and HER2-Warwick are pooled *into* the 1,433-WSI pipeline, so this is not an external number at all; the authors say so.
- **Ektefaie 2021** patient-level 4-class top-1 **0.654** and **Jaber 2020** patient-level **67.27%** are accuracies, not AUROCs, and neither reports any external PAM50 number despite Ektefaie 2021 having two external cohorts available.
- Everything omics-only (Benkirane 2023 0.9828, Li C 2026 0.965, Tang X 2024 0.83, Choi & Chae 2023 0.891 acc, Wang T 2021 0.829 acc, Qiu 2026 F1 0.872) belongs next to our RNA-only 0.988, not next to 0.888. Everything binary (Shandiz 2025, Abdullakutty 2026, Kurian 2025, Lee 2024, Huang 2023, Couture 2018) and everything IHC-labelled (Liu Hong 2022, Kiraz 2025, Li B 2026, Ben Rabah 2025, Zhang T 2023) is a different task.
- **Wang Y 2021** and **Pizurica 2024 (SEQUOIA)** report no subtype metric at all, but they bound the H&E→RNA channel our fusion depends on: Wang Y 2021 recovers a significant correlation for 52.75% of 17,695 genes in validation yet only 5.80% reach R² > 0.2, and SEQUOIA's mean per-gene Pearson is 0.504 in its best configuration. The transcriptome is partially readable from H&E, weakly and per-gene. That is the quantitative reason an image branch cannot substitute for a measured RNA branch.

**The fusion result (0.981 gated fusion < 0.988 RNA-only) is the field's modal finding, not an anomaly.** Four independent groups report the same thing: Rajalekshmi 2026 (no fusion arm beat molecular-only under a matched 5-fold protocol); Hezil 2026a (concatenation 85.31%, gated 85.12%, both well below RNA-only 92.11%); Abdullakutty 2026 ("conventional early fusion provides no significant improvement"); Benkirane 2025 (multi-omics 98.3 ± 1.0 → WSI+multi-omics 98.7 ± 1.1, a 0.4-point gain inside one standard deviation). Only two papers claim a clear fusion win over an RNA baseline for a PAM50-like target: Hezil 2026a's cross-attention/routing (94.23%/94.93% vs 92.11%) on a **138-sample single split** by a group whose released code disagrees with its own paper on the class count, and Shandiz 2025 (binary, no sample size at all, unnamed dataset, no CIs). That is a thin basis for the field's prevailing assumption. Our ablation showing the gate decides on RNA alone is the mechanistic explanation nobody else has published for a result four other groups have already observed.

**Zhang T 2023 is the honest counterexample, and it sharpens the claim rather than weakening it.** MDL-IIA's mammography + ultrasound fusion beats both of its unimodal arms decisively (MCC 0.837 vs 0.731 and 0.744, p < 0.001 on a 672-case held-out test with CIs). So fusion is not inherently useless. What distinguishes it from every imaging+RNA result is that its two modalities are *comparably weak and genuinely complementary*: neither mammography nor ultrasound is a measurement of the receptor label, each contributes non-redundant evidence, and the fusion has something to arbitrate. Imaging+RNA→PAM50 has the opposite structure — one modality is a lossy proxy for the label and the other is very nearly the assay that defines it, so any competent gate learns to ignore the image. Our ablation is a direct instrument reading of that asymmetry. Note also that MDL-IIA is IHC-surrogate, single-centre and externally unvalidated, so its own generalisation is unknown.

**The clinicopathology null is also corroborated.** Furtney 2023's own ablation shows *removing* the clinical subgraph **improves** TCGA performance (micro 0.871 → 0.880, macro 0.772 → 0.779). Couture 2018's grade-weighted ER model is worse than the unweighted one (80% vs 84%). Rajalekshmi 2026's clinical-only arm reaches 0.44–0.50 accuracy against molecular-only 0.87–0.89. The one counterpoint that must be reported honestly is **Amer 2025**, where clinical is the *strongest* single modality (accuracy 70.43%, macro-AUC 0.8522) — but the contents of that EHR vector are not specified, and if it encodes ER/PR/HER2 status the comparison is circular in exactly the way our own clinicopath analysis flagged.

**Net positioning.** This project is not the best number in the table and should not claim to be. It is, on the evidence assembled here, the only pipeline that (a) evaluates a WSI+RNA fusion model on an independent cohort with a PAM50-specific metric, (b) reports the fusion-vs-RNA comparison as a negative result with an ablation explaining the mechanism, and (c) reports case-level rather than slide- or patch-level external metrics. The contribution is methodological honesty on an axis where the literature is systematically weak, not a state-of-the-art AUROC.

## 7. Open gaps
Ranked by how open they are and how cheaply this project can close them.

**1. No external, never-trained-on, PAM50-specific evaluation of a WSI+RNA fusion model exists.** Of the 8 H&E+RNA papers, six report no external cohort at all or leave it NR (Hezil 2026a explicitly: "single TCGA-BRCA cohort, single random split, no independent cohort and no tissue-source-site holdout"; also Abdullakutty 2026, Shandiz 2025, Shukla 2025, Zhang Z 2024, Hezil 2026b). Benkirane 2025 has an external cohort — private IALT, n=544 — but it is NSCLC survival, not breast subtype. Shah & Patel 2026 has CPTAC in protocol E1 but reports only a pooled "Overall AUC 0.82" across five heterogeneous endpoints, with no PAM50 breakdown. Crucially, this is a choice rather than a data limitation: the WSI→expression tier routinely externally validates (Wang Y 2021 on ABiM, n=350, 908/995 genes replicating; SEQUOIA on CPTAC across 7 cancer types plus a 287-slide Tempus cohort), and CPTAC-BRCA is used as a held-out set for WSI-only PAM50 by Fernandez-Romero 2026, Borji 2026 and Zhang X 2025. Only the fusion papers skip it. This project's external CPTAC fusion number, negative though it is, has no published counterpart.

**2. Nobody has run a properly powered fusion-vs-RNA-only head-to-head.** The two claimed wins rest on n=138 with no CIs (Hezil 2026a) and on an unnamed dataset with no sample size (Shandiz 2025). The four null results (Rajalekshmi 2026, Abdullakutty 2026's early-fusion arm, Hezil 2026a's own concat/gated arms, Benkirane 2025's 0.4-point delta) are each single-protocol. Zhang T 2023 shows exactly what the missing experiment looks like — a 672-case held-out test, bootstrap CIs on every arm, and explicit significance tests of multimodal against each unimodal baseline (p < 0.001) — but it does it on mammography + ultrasound. No imaging+RNA PAM50 paper meets that standard, and a multi-seed, CI-bearing, matched-preprocessing comparison of RNA-only against each fusion operator on one cohort does not exist in this table.

**3. The PAM50-gene leakage experiment has never been run.** No row reports excluding the PAM50 genes from RNA inputs. The circumstantial evidence that the transcriptomic ceiling is the assay reproducing itself is now strong from three directions: direct-read omics models cluster at 0.96–0.98 AUROC (Benkirane 2023 0.9828, Rajalekshmi 2026 0.97–0.99, Li C 2026 0.965, our RNA-only 0.988); a model that image-encodes the same omics before reading it drops to 0.83 ± 0.07 (Tang X 2024); and a model whose expression is *predicted* rather than measured falls to 0.55 accuracy / 0.54 F1 despite recovering PAM50 genes among its best-predicted set (Mondol 2023, 13 of the top 20). Ektefaie 2021's ESR1-alone ER AUC of 0.883 is the single-gene version of the same point. The decisive experiment — drop the 50 genes, retrain, re-measure the fusion delta — is still unpublished. This remains the cheapest genuinely novel result available.

**4. HER2-enriched transfer is unsolved and no published intervention fixes it.** Fernandez-Romero 2026: RPD = 1.000, complete collapse, across *all* models on CPTAC. Tafavvoghi 2025: HER2-enriched F1 0.545, recall 0.469, AUPRC 0.64 (0.48–0.78) — the worst class by a wide margin. Patel 2025: HER2+ F1 = 0 for all four concatenation baselines. Mondol 2023: HER2 per-class AUROC 0.78 from predicted expression. Tang X 2024: HER2 0.89 ± 0.07, the widest standard deviation of its four classes, on 74 HER2 samples. Fernandez-Romero 2026's IHC HER2 PR-AUC is 0.399 internal / 0.219 external even with Virchow v2. Qiu 2026 needs 5-shot MAML (F1 0.782) and MolGAN augmentation (HER2 AUC 0.935) to move it, on transcriptomics with no imaging. The single exception is Ming 2022b, where HER2E is the *only* class whose external AUC exceeds its internal one (0.7933 → 0.809) — from a two-feature DCE-MRI radiomics classifier on 72 patients, which is a sample-size artefact, not a method. Our external HER2-enriched recall of 0.00 is therefore expected, and nobody has published a HER2-E-specific intervention that survives a cohort change.

**5. Normal-like inclusion is decided arbitrarily and its cost has never been measured.** Thirteen papers drop it, seven keep it, seven never say, and two contradict themselves (Hezil 2026a text-vs-code; Liu H 2024's four output nodes in Methods against "all five subtypes" in Results). Only Tang X 2024 gives any rationale for dropping it. The same TCGA-BRCA yields Normal-like counts from 35 (Omran 2025) to 115 (Wang T 2021). **No paper reports the same model under both label spaces on the same split**, so the entire 4-vs-5-class accuracy gap in this literature is unquantified. This is a one-experiment gap.

**6. Prediction-unit effects are never controlled.** Jaber 2020 is the only paper reporting patch/slide/patient for one model (58.62 / 66.1 / 67.27%), Phan 2021 shows patch 0.78 → slide 0.913, and Borji 2026 publishes patch-indexed external metrics that read as case-level results. Until someone publishes a level-controlled table, every cross-paper comparison in this field — including the ones in Section 3 — is uncalibrated by an unknown amount that Jaber 2020 suggests is around 9 points.

**7. Encoder contribution, aggregator contribution and fusion contribution have never been factorially separated.** Fernandez-Romero 2026 shows encoder choice alone spans macro-F1 0.342–0.575 internally and *reorders completely* externally (Prov-GigaPath ranks 1st externally at 0.379 while UNI-2 drops from 1st internally to 7th at 0.325). Kaczmarzyk 2026 finds the same instability on ROR-P: H-optimus-0 is best on CBCS regression and falls *below* ResNet50 on TCGA. SEQUOIA shows the aggregator matters comparably even with the encoder fixed: with UNI features held constant, mean per-gene Pearson is 0.504 for its linearized transformer against 0.428 for an MLP and 0.419 for a standard transformer. Yet every fusion paper fixes both (ResNet50 in Shah & Patel 2026 and Shandiz 2025, ViT in Shukla 2025) and attributes the entire gain to the fusion operator. No encoder × aggregator × fusion-operator grid exists.

**8. Site/scanner confounding is tested in omics but not in histology.** Li C 2026 runs a site-stratified holdout across five TCGA source sites (worst-site accuracy 0.841 → 0.872) and Wang T 2021 mentions a within-TCGA cross-institution split with numbers NR. On the imaging side, only Ming 2022b deliberately changes scanner between cohorts (Siemens 3T discovery → 1.5T GE external), and Zhang T 2023 concedes its single mammography system and single ultrasound system as a headline limitation. **No H&E PAM50 paper in this table performs a tissue-source-site holdout**, and Hezil 2026a explicitly flags its absence. Given that Fernandez-Romero 2026 attributes 80.0% of transfer degradation to staining and feature-space divergence, site holdout is the obvious missing internal control.

**9. Calibration is reported only where imaging is absent, and clinical utility is unestablished.** ECE appears in exactly two rows — Hezil 2026a (RNA-only 0.118, routing 0.061) and Li C 2026 (0.031) — both omics-side. Not one WSI-only PAM50 paper in this table reports a calibration metric, despite calibration being the thing that decides whether a subtype prediction can be used for triage; Zhang T 2023 comes closest by putting 95% CIs on every reported figure, which is still not calibration. Only two papers benchmark against humans at all: Liu Hong 2022 (a 9-pathologist reader study on 99 randomly selected test WSIs, against its own four-class accuracy of 57.8%) and Zhang T 2023 (a 168-case observer study on 25% of its test cohort). Only Lee 2024 and Kaczmarzyk 2026 tie image-derived scores to actual recurrence outcomes. Clinical utility for image-based PAM50 remains entirely unestablished.
